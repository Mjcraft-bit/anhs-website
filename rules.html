Based on DepEd Order No. 006, S. 2026 — Guidelines on Ensuring a Safe and Motivating Learning Environment.

Disciplinary Intervention Levels

Bullying incidents are handled according to their nature, gravity, and severity. All reports are treated with fairness and confidentiality.

First Level — Minor Bullying Acts

Precursors to bullying — behaviors that may lead to more serious issues if not corrected.

Examples:

  • Uttering profanities or hurtful words against a learner
  • Disruptive behavior or pranks that disturb others
  • Taking or using another's belongings without permission
  • Unwanted physical contact that causes discomfort but no injury
  • Fighting that does not result in physical harm

Penalties:

  • First Offense: Written reprimand + summons to parents
  • Second Offense: Suspension for 3 days
  • Third or Subsequent Offense: Suspension for 5 days

Second Level — Serious Bullying Acts

Behaviors that harm, threaten, or degrade others — these are taken seriously.

Examples:

  • Stalking or repeatedly following someone
  • Catcalling, wolf-whistling, or making unwanted comments
  • Spreading rumors, sharing private photos, or revealing personal details
  • Insulting based on gender, family, or identity
  • Threatening, intimidating, or causing slight physical injury

Penalties:

  • First Offense: Suspension for 5 days + referral to Social Welfare Office
  • Second Offense: Non-readmission to the school
  • Third or Subsequent Offense: Permanent exclusion

Third Level — Severe Bullying Acts

Cannot be resolved in the classroom — these cause serious harm and have major consequences.

Examples:

  • Physical assault causing injury to another student
  • Severe verbal abuse, hate remarks, or body shaming
  • Demanding money, stealing, or damaging property
  • Posting harmful photos or videos online to embarrass someone
  • Repeated severe acts despite previous warnings

Penalties:

  • First Offense: Exclusion from the school — may not join any school activities
  • These acts may lead to permanent loss of rights and privileges

How Incidents Are Handled

  1. Report the incident to the School Head or Guidance Counselor
  2. Student safety is ensured — those involved are separated
  3. Parents or guardians of all students involved are informed
  4. Students are interviewed separately to find the truth
  5. The situation is assessed and appropriate support is provided
  6. Counseling or intervention programs are arranged as needed
